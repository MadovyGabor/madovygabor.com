Fire Detection and Alarm System (FDAS): Bosch AVENAR panel 2000

Life-safety installation — educational facility (kindergarten & school), Tvrdošovce, Slovakia

01 / Overview & Standards

Installation, LSN loop programming and building integration of the fire detection and alarm system (FDAS; Slovak: elektronická požiarna signalizácia, EPS) for an educational facility (kindergarten and school) in Tvrdošovce, Slovakia. The FDAS controller is a Bosch AVENAR panel 2000; door holders, the main power distribution and a GSM dialer are controlled by an RML 0008 A relay module on the panel's internal rail.

  • Readable alarms: the panel shows functional zones and room names, not raw LSN bus addresses.
  • Fail-safe controls: escape doors release on alarm and on loss of power.
  • Battery sizing to EN 54-4: 24 h standby plus 30 min alarm.

04 / Cause & Effect

Building functions are driven by the RML 0008 A relay module on the panel rail.

Output Normal state On fire alarm On power loss
Door holder magnets Energised, escape doors held Relay cuts the supply, doors release mechanically Released (fail-safe)
Main distribution Shunt trip idle Shunt trip (vyrážacia spúšť) fires, non-essential 230/400 V circuits are switched off Not applicable
GSM dialer Dry NC loop closed Loop opens, the dialer sends the alarm Stays powered from the battery-backed AUX rail
GSM input and supply

The dialer sits inside the metal panel cabinet, so the dry NC loop never leaves the enclosure and a cable break is practically excluded; any opening of the loop triggers it immediately. It is powered from the 24 V AUX rail through an isolated Mean Well DDR-15G-12 (24 V → 12 V), not from a single battery, which would unbalance the series battery set.

05 / Technical Challenges & Troubleshooting

01

Working in a closed industrial system

Approach
Context

Bosch AVENAR, RPS and LSN are closed life-safety products. Unlike open-source web technology there is minimal public documentation, no Stack Overflow coverage and no useful AI training data on their internal logic or error codes.

check_circle Approach

Commissioning relied on reading the engineering schematics and manufacturer documentation, multimeter measurements on the loop, and hands-on fault isolation.

02

Rail module order and bus power

Panel hardware
Symptom & root cause

The panel failed its initial internal hardware validation. The modules were seated in the wrong order on the internal rail (PRS-0002A).

The panel backplane enforces a fixed physical order: the battery controller feeds power into the rail, and the other modules are arranged by current draw to avoid voltage drops on the internal DC bus and logic arbitration faults.

check_circle Fix

Modules re-seated in the required order: FPE-8000-BCM in slot 1 next to the power feed, then the LSN 0300 A loop controller, then the RML 0008 A relay module. Hardware validation passed.

Rail order after correction 
rail position   module          role
1               FPE-8000-BCM    battery controller · power feed
2               LSN 0300 A      LSN loop controller
3               RML 0008 A      8 relay outputs
03

Base sounder wiring: schematic vs. hardware

Wiring
Symptom & root cause

The FNM-420U sounder bases stayed silent and caused a continuous loop fault.

The schematic required a manual jumper between two base terminals. The current hardware revision bridges these internally, so the obsolete jumper interfered with LSN line monitoring.

check_circle Fix

Removed the jumper and returned to a clean LSN line. The sounders became addressable immediately and the line fault cleared.

04

Locating a loop fault with T-tap mode

Diagnostics
Symptom & root cause

The closed LSN ring would not come up; devices reported short circuits and lost communication.

In a ring the panel communicates from both ends, which hides where the fault actually is.

check_circle Fix
  1. Panel temporarily switched to open (T-tap / stub) polling.
  2. Devices were addressed one after another along the cable; the scan stopped at the faulty point.
  3. At that detector the terminal screw had pinched the insulation instead of the bare conductor. Re-stripped 8–10 mm, rescanned to the last device and closed the ring again in RPS.
05

BCM energy limit exceeded (72 h vs. 24 h)

Power
Symptom & root cause

RPS reported:

Fatal: Energy Zone 1 power consumption exceeds the maximum limit (FPE-8000-BCM)

Standby time had been set to 72 h. The standby current is 0.46 A and the battery set is 2× 12 V 26 Ah in series, which gives 24 V 26 Ah — capacity does not add up in series.

check_circle Fix

Standby time set back to 24 h + 30 min alarm, the standard requirement for this type of building. The RPS consistency check passed.

EN 54-4 battery check 0.8 = usable capacity factor 
usable capacity   26 Ah × 0.8          = 20.8 Ah
72 h standby      0.46 A × 72 h        = 33.1 Ah   > 20.8 Ah   ✗
max. at 72 h      20.8 Ah ÷ 72 h       ≈ 0.29 A    < 0.46 A    ✗
24 h standby      0.46 A × 24 h        = 11.0 Ah   + 30 min alarm  ✓
